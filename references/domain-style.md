# Structural and earthquake engineering profile

Use this profile to focus review, not to force one research vocabulary onto every manuscript.

## Terminology checkpoints

- Distinguish `self-centering`, `recentering`, and `rocking`; explain the mechanism instead of using them as synonyms.
- Distinguish free rocking, controlled rocking, rocking isolation, uplifting, and base rocking.
- Distinguish residual displacement, residual interstory drift, residual drift ratio, and permanent deformation.
- Distinguish stiffness, strength, deformation capacity, ductility, energy-dissipation capacity, collapse capacity, and collapse resistance.
- Distinguish hazard, demand, capacity, fragility, vulnerability, loss, resilience, repairability, and functional recovery.
- Define the reference coordinate, sign convention, story index, normalization, and units for every important symbol.
- State whether `drift` means story displacement, interstory drift, roof drift, or a normalized ratio.
- Keep `record`, `ground motion`, `ground-motion component`, and `earthquake event` distinct.
- State whether reported values are median, mean, percentile, maximum, envelope, or a single record response.

## Method and evidence checkpoints

- Verify specimen scale, similitude laws, boundary conditions, axial load, post-tensioning force, loading protocol, instrumentation, and failure criteria.
- For numerical models, verify constitutive laws, deterioration, contact/uplift, damping, convergence, calibration, record scaling, and uncertainty treatment.
- For fragility or loss studies, verify intensity measure, engineering demand parameter, limit states, collapse treatment, uncertainty components, and conditioning assumptions.
- For self-centering systems, trace restoring force, energy dissipation, decompression/uplift, gap opening, fuse activation, tendon/device limits, residual response, and replaceability.
- Treat `damage-free`, `zero damage`, `immediate occupancy`, `negligible residual drift`, and `full recovery` as strong claims requiring explicit evidence and scope.

## Quantitative cross-checks

Compare abstract, main text, tables, figure labels, captions, and conclusions for specimen counts, periods, damping ratios, scale factors, drift limits, force levels, material properties, uncertainty values, and improvement percentages. Recalculate simple percentages when the underlying values are available. Do not infer data from an unreadable plot.

Build a reproducible quantitative ledger rather than scanning isolated numbers:

- Record every important symbol at first definition, its meaning, unit or dimension, index scope, sign convention, and later redefinitions. Keep local summation/dummy indices separate from manuscript-wide symbols.
- Normalize compatible units before comparison and preserve the original text. Treat an incompatible dimension as a formal finding only when the symbol meaning or profile makes the expected dimension explicit.
- Link repeated specimen properties, cross-section dimensions, material strengths, periods, damping ratios, loads, drifts, and response peaks across equations, prose, tables, captions, and conclusions.
- Recalculate explicit `from A to B` changes, percentages, means, error measures, and simple unit conversions. Record operands, normalized units, formula, tolerance, and result.
- Compare summary numbers in the abstract and conclusion with the same metric, entity, hazard level, statistic, and baseline in the results. A similar number with a different denominator, load case, percentile, or specimen is not a valid match.
- Treat values visually estimated from a figure, OCR output, ambiguous cross-section matches, and formulas with unresolved symbol dimensions as review candidates until a rendered figure or author-defined mapping confirms them.

## Engineering-standard applicability

- Record the complete designation and edition at every controlling use. Distinguish publication date from the edition adopted by the governing code, jurisdiction, project date, or experimental protocol.
- For Eurocodes, record the exact part, national adoption, National Annex, and Nationally Determined Parameters where applicable. For Chinese standards, preserve `GB` versus `GB/T` and `JGJ` versus `JGJ/T`.
- For every cited clause, equation, table, or figure, build a provision card containing the exact edition, identifier, direct source location, symbol mapping, unit system, coefficients, limits, exceptions, referenced provisions, and amendments/errata checked.
- Test scope against structural material and system, seismic force-resisting system, hazard and design category, analysis method, limit state, member/component type, and jurisdiction. A clause that exists can still be inapplicable.
- Do not call an older edition wrong merely because a newer publication exists. Do not call a locator or formula correct from memory, a search snippet, or a secondary design guide.

## Writing profile

Prefer precise, bounded claims. State the comparison baseline and hazard level. Use active voice when it improves attribution, but do not rewrite established technical conventions merely to enforce a style preference. Avoid promotional adjectives such as `excellent`, `remarkable`, `superior`, or `novel` unless followed by a measurable basis.
